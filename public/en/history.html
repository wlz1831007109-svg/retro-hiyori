Thirteen gateways into Chinese painting history.

Before the 3rd century

Origins: Prehistory to Han

From painted pottery and silk paintings to tomb murals and pictorial stones, early images were closely tied to ritual, burial, cosmology and record-keeping. These traditions formed important foundations for later Chinese painting.

Six Dynasties

Figure painting, expressive line and theories of spirit resonance developed rapidly. Landscape gradually moved from a background element toward an independent subject of appreciation.

Figure lineSpirit resonanceEarly painting theory

Sui

The Sui reunited north and south and formed a bridge into Tang visual culture. Early blue-and-green landscape is especially important when considering the development of independent landscape painting.

Blue-and-green landscapeCultural synthesisTransition to Tang
Explore this period →618–907

Tang

Court figures, Buddhist painting, horses, birds-and-flowers and landscape all flourished. Rich color and confident figure design reflect the cosmopolitan culture of the Tang empire.

Court figure paintingBuddhist imageryHorses and landscape
Explore this period →907–960

Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms

Northern monumental landscape and the softer, humid landscapes of Jiangnan developed distinctive languages. Major bird-and-flower traditions also took shape, preparing the ground for Song painting.

Northern & southern landscapeJiangnan atmosphereBird-and-flower traditions
Explore this period →960–1127

Northern Song

The imperial academy and scholar-official culture developed in parallel. Monumental landscape, detailed bird-and-flower painting and literati ideas made the Northern Song one of the great peaks of Chinese painting.

Monumental landscapeImperial academyScholar-official culture
Explore this period →1127–1279

Southern Song

Centered on Lin’an, court painting favored poetic small scenes, atmospheric space and compositions that make powerful use of empty areas. Chan-associated ink painting also developed distinctive forms.

Poetic spaceOne-corner compositionAtmospheric ink
Explore this period →1271–1368

Yuan

Literati painting became increasingly central. Calligraphic brushwork, engagement with older styles and personal self-expression reshaped the meaning of painting for generations to come.

Literati paintingCalligraphic brushworkArchaism and self-expression
Explore this period →1368–1644

Ming

Court painters, the Zhe School, the Wu School and many independent artists coexisted. Collecting, connoisseurship and literati theory expanded alongside urban culture.

Wu SchoolZhe SchoolUrban collecting culture
Explore this period →1644–1912

Qing

Orthodox landscape lineages, individualist monks, regional schools, the Yangzhou Eccentrics and court painting created a highly plural visual world.

Orthodox lineagesIndividualists and regional schoolsCourt and commercial culture

Late Qing & Shanghai School

Expanding urban markets encouraged bold brushwork, accessible subjects and new relationships between artists, collectors and commerce. The Shanghai School became especially influential.

Modern China

Traditional Chinese painting, Western art, modern art education and social change intersected. Artists debated how brush-and-ink traditions could respond to a rapidly changing modern world.

FORMATS / HOW TO SEE

How Chinese painting & calligraphy are viewed.

The experience changes with the format: a hanging scroll is seen on the wall, a handscroll unfolds over time, and an album is viewed closely page by page.

SIX KEY FORMATS

Six forms worth knowing first.

DISPLAY ON A WALL

Hanging scroll / vertical scroll

A vertical work mounted for hanging. The painting, mounting, surrounding wall and hanging height together shape the experience.

UNFOLD BY HAND

Handscroll

A long horizontal work viewed gradually from right to left. Time and movement become part of the composition.

VIEW CLOSELY

Album leaves

Small works bound or grouped as a set, ideal for close attention to brushwork, inscriptions and seals.

MULTI-PANEL DISPLAY

Screen strips / four-panel sets

Several vertical panels form one decorative ensemble, often used for seasons, flowers-and-birds or landscapes.

CURVED PICTURE PLANE

Fan painting

A compact composition designed for a fan-shaped surface, with branches, figures or landscape responding to the arc.

READ AS A PAIR

Couplets

Two vertical calligraphic works displayed together. Meaning, character structure and spatial symmetry are appreciated as a pair.

MORE FORMATS

Other common forms.

Horizontal hanging work

A wide-format display suited to landscape and calligraphy.

Unmounted artwork sheet

The painting or calligraphy before mounting; it can later be framed or mounted as a scroll.

Boxed sets & small works

Albums, groups of works and compact pieces can be stored, collected and displayed in different ways.

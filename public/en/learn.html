BEGINNER’S GUIDE

Getting started with Chinese painting & calligraphy.

You do not need specialist vocabulary to begin. Start with four things: subject, brushwork, format and inscriptions. These simple questions make a work much easier to read.

01

Look beyond the image alone.

Chinese painting and calligraphy can include painted imagery, poems, inscriptions, calligraphy, seals and mounting. Together they create a complete viewing space.

Landscape

Read mountains, water, clouds and trees as a space through which the eye can travel.

Bird-and-flower

Plants, birds, insects and fish can combine observation, seasonal feeling and auspicious meaning.

Figure painting

Look at posture and drapery lines before focusing only on the face.

02

Check “hand-painted” versus “craft-produced.”

Retro日和 uses hand-painted / hand-written only for works made by hand with brush, ink or pigments. Other production methods are labeled craft-produced work. An artist name or signature-like mark is not, by itself, proof of authorship.

03

Gongbi and xieyi are different modes, not rigid boxes.

Gongbi tends toward controlled outlines and layered detail. Xieyi often uses speed, omission and expressive ink. Many works sit somewhere between these poles.

04

Paper and silk change the look of ink and color.

Paper can emphasize absorption, wet–dry variation and broken ink; silk can support fine lines and delicate layers of color. Surface preparation and age matter as much as the basic material name.

05

Read inscriptions and seals as part of the composition.

Names, poems, dates and seals can balance empty space and create a rhythm between painting and writing. A name on the work is a clue—not an automatic authenticity certificate.

06

Why are people often tiny in landscape painting?

Small travelers, pavilions and paths establish scale. They also invite the viewer to imagine moving through the mountains rather than looking at nature from a fixed distance.
